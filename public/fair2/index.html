How the current suggestion is calculated

All five core items must be answered: cleanliness, air, safety, climate and privacy. All positive with no reported failure of a promised amenity suggests 5; one concern suggests 4; two or more suggest 3. A safety or privacy concern stops automatic suggestions and prompts a report. These are demo rules, not an AI model. Riders may choose any final rating from 1 to 5. Charging is advertised in the demo; water and other extras are optional.

Economics that hold up.

Decision model · Not a live quote
Working conclusion: a human-driver service can compete on selected trips, but a $2 all-in take does not cover the base-case costs. A $2 platform fee plus transparent cost recovery may work at sufficient volume. No driver or rider penalties are counted as revenue here.
Worked example · HOU → Katy · Standard sedan

A fare to test, with the driver protected.

$57.90 before optional tip

Compared with the rider-reported $63.61 Lyft fare: $5.71 less (8.98%). Modeled driver payout: $46.67 before vehicle expenses and tax. This is a test price, not a live quote or evidence of this Lyft driver’s earnings.

Assumptions: 75 total driver minutes, 48 total miles, $0.45 vehicle cost/mile, $4 commercial insurance, $3.25 airport/toll/tax allowance, card processing at 2.9% + $0.30 and a $2 Fair2 fee. Time and distance are scenario inputs, not a verified route measurement. Insurance and airport/toll/tax amounts need actual quotes and receipts.

Driver net: about $20.06/hour before personal tax. Fair2 retains $0.65 per ride after $1.35 of assumed variable costs, before fixed overhead. No tips, penalties or subsidies fund this case. A $54.90 fare would reduce modeled driver net to $17.73/hour.

Add 30 empty miles and 45 unpaid minutes after drop-off, and driver net falls to about $5.79/hour. Favor a driver heading toward Katy or with a paid onward trip; price the extra work if neither is available. A robotaxi price comparison needs a real same-route, same-time quote.

What the assumptions include

Total time includes the passenger ride, pickup, idle time and repositioning. All miles include paid and unpaid travel. Vehicle cost includes fuel, wear, depreciation and the driver's own insurance; commercial platform coverage is modeled separately. Net hourly is after vehicle costs, before personal tax.

Other variable costs default to $0.60 support/fraud/quality review, $0.10 maps/technology, $0.15 onboarding/payouts and $0.50 amortized acquisition. These, the $2 insurance allowance, $50,000 monthly overhead and driver costs are assumptions—not supplier quotes. Enter all applicable taxes, tolls and airport fees in the allowance field. The comparison quote must include its own applicable charges; exclude optional tips from both fares. XXL costs include a higher vehicle allowance; actual seating and child-seat configurations need verification.

Compare the same origin, destination, booking time, taxes, capacity and wait time. The $20 robotaxi input is hypothetical. Historical averages below are not a substitute for a current route quote. Seven-seat XXL needs a like-capacity comparison.

Does this trip work?

Launch decision

Start with scheduled family, airport and business trips in a compact service area. Validate insurance and driver supply before promising prices. Use the robotaxi price as a ceiling only when it is above the fully costed floor; otherwise decline the price match or offer a different service.

A platform that depends on poor ratings to earn a margin has the wrong incentive. The five-star case must cover costs. The strict recruitment and removal policy can reduce available drivers, increase pickup times and raise this fare floor.
